A fan is chosen for a duty point: the airflow the system needs (m³/h) and the static pressure the fan has to overcome at that airflow (Pa).

The pressure comes from the ductwork: duct length, bends, grilles, filters and silencers. A short duct with a grille needs 50-150 Pa; a supply system with a filter and a heater easily needs 300-500 Pa.

Read the fan curve at the required airflow. The fan fits if its pressure at that airflow is at least the required pressure. Keep a margin of 10-20%: filters load up with dust and the pressure grows over time. A much larger margin means a noisier, more expensive fan that runs throttled.

If the customer only knows the room size, use air changes: an office needs about 3-4 air changes per hour, a kitchen 10-15, a warehouse 1-2. Airflow = room volume × air changes.
